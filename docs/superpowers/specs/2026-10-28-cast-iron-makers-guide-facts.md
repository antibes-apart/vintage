# Cast iron makers guide — fact checklist (for owner review)

Researched 2026-10-28. Every source below was opened and the fact read on it. Strength:
**strong** = official/institutional/court/register source or two independent good sources;
**single** = one acceptable source (or several copying one); **conflicting** = good sources disagree;
**weak** = Wikipedia or similar only. Marketplaces, auction lots, forums and uncited blogs were not used.

**How to review:** for each row, keep ✅, strike ❌, or correct it. The "Page" column is my proposal:
**yes** = state plainly, **hedged** = state with "according to…" / "sources differ", **no** = leave out.

**Owner decision, 2026-10-28:** "write the page" — the "Page" column is approved as proposed. Where it says
"hedged, or no" (CO3, DE4, X4) the fact is left out.

**Re-check before writing (2026-10-28):** the assistant re-read the key sources directly. CO4 (Doufeu) was
unsupported and is removed; CO7 could not be re-verified and is left out; ST1 now separates the founding place
from the current head office, which sources disagree on; FO5 is a new finding kept off the page until reviewed.

## Left out (seen, not supported)

- Cousances as "the first cast-iron cookware foundry in the world": Le Creuset marketing superlative.
- Doufeu "designed in 1934": marketplace listing only.
- Le Creuset bought Descoware's company or moulds: uncited blogs; the only detailed account mentions enamel rights only.
- Fontignac "since 1926", "founded 1850 by Louis Fontignac", "Villedieu-les-Poêles": marketplace text or unsourced, contradictory articles.
- Staub's first cocotte "in a dormant artillery factory"; Zwilling deal "June 2008": not confirmed on an opened page.
- DRU "Confetti" designed by Wim Gilles: vintage-shop pages only.
- DRU cookware ended in the 1960s because of natural gas: Wikipedia only, contradicted by the 1973/1982 dates.
- Le Creuset founder spelt "Auberg": not verifiable (our Le Creuset guide uses "Aubecq").
